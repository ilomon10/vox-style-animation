# How learning London changes a cab driver's brain
// Example project of the framework, in the scrapbook look measured on a Vox brand piece
// (analysis/vox-sample/vox_braintravel.md). Every claim is sourced in RESEARCH.md.
// Build it: tools/py tools/examples/build_example.py example-cab-brain

[BEAT recipe=ChapterCard anchor="To"]
To drive a black cab in London, you have to pass a test called the Knowledge.
[BEAT recipe=PaperStat anchor="It"]
It covers about *twenty-five thousand* streets, all within six miles of Charing Cross.
[BEAT recipe=SpecimenInterview anchor="On"]
On average, learning them takes about three years.

[PAUSE 0.8]

[BEAT recipe=ChapterCard anchor="In"]
In two thousand, neuroscientists scanned the brains of London cab drivers.
[BEAT recipe=StampedStat anchor="Sixteen"]
*Sixteen* of them, and fifty men who did not drive taxis.
[BEAT recipe=SpecimenPlate anchor="The"]
The back of the drivers' hippocampus, a region for memory and navigation, was *larger*.
And the longer they had driven, the larger it was.

[PAUSE 0.8]

[BEAT recipe=ChapterCard anchor="But"]
But were they simply born that way?
[BEAT recipe=PaperStat anchor="A"]
A study published in two thousand eleven followed *seventy-nine* trainees through the Knowledge.
[BEAT recipe=SpecimenCompare anchor="The"]
The ones who qualified grew more grey matter there.
The ones who did not, and men who never trained, showed no such change.

[PAUSE 0.6]

[BEAT recipe=ChapterCard anchor="Learning"]
Learning a city, it seems, can reshape the brain that learns it.
