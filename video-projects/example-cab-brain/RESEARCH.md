# Research: How learning London changes a cab driver's brain

Example project of the framework, made in the motion-graphics style measured on a Vox brand piece
(`analysis/vox-sample/vox_braintravel.md`). Every line of the narration rests on a fact below; nothing else is claimed.
Checked 2026-11-17 by web search.

| # | Fact used in the script | Source(s) | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | London's black-cab licence test, "the Knowledge", covers about 25,000 streets within a six-mile radius of Charing Cross. | LEVC, "The Knowledge", https://www.levc.com/company/the-knowledge/; National Geographic (August 2014), https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/140808-london-cabbies-knowledge-cabs-hansom-uber-hippocampus-livery | The street count is the same across sources. |
| 2 | Learning it takes about three years on average. | LEVC (above): it says the average time to complete the Knowledge is three years | Other sources give 2–4 years: the script says "about three years". |
| 3 | In 2000, Maguire and colleagues scanned 16 licensed London taxi drivers and 50 men who did not drive taxis. The back (posterior) part of the drivers' hippocampi was larger. | Maguire et al., "Navigation-related structural change in the hippocampi of taxi drivers", PNAS 97(8):4398–4403 (2000), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC18253 | |
| 4 | The longer someone had driven a taxi, the larger their posterior hippocampus. | Maguire et al. 2000 (above) | A correlation across drivers. |
| 5 | The 2000 study could not rule out that people with a larger posterior hippocampus are the ones who become taxi drivers. | Terrazas & McNaughton, commentary in the same PNAS issue, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC34310 | The script asks "were they born that way?" |
| 6 | In 2011, Woollett and Maguire followed 79 trainees through the Knowledge (with 31 men who were not training). The trainees who qualified gained grey matter in the posterior hippocampus; those who did not qualify, and the controls, showed no such change. | Woollett & Maguire, "Acquiring 'the Knowledge' of London's layout drives structural brain changes", Current Biology (December 2011), doi:10.1016/j.cub.2011.11.018, https://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/Maguire/WoollettMaguireCBDec2011.pdf | 39 of the 79 qualified. |
| 7 | The hippocampus has a central role in memory and spatial navigation. | Woollett & Maguire 2011 and Maguire et al. 2000 (above) | |

Not used: the pass rate (sources give "about one in five" without a clear origin) and the number of landmarks (20,000 to 60,000 depending on the source).
